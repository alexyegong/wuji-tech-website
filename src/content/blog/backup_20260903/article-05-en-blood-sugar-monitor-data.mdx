# Blood Sugar Monitoring: Fasting Blood Sugar 7.05, Then 5.58/5.47/5.02/5.45/5.71; OGTT 2h Postprandial Results 5.62/10.13/6.84—Is It Safe?

> **Core Insight:** Fasting blood sugar looks good, but the Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) exposes a key issue—**the second postprandial 2-hour blood sugar reached 10.13 mmol/L, placing it in the "impaired glucose tolerance" (IGT) range.** This isn't diabetes, but it's not entirely normal either. You are standing at a crossroads.

---

## 1. Conclusion: Don't Relax Yet, But Don't Panic

Your situation can be summarized as:
- **Fasting Blood Sugar:** Generally normal, though the initial value of 7.05 mmol/L was right at the diagnostic threshold for diabetes.
- **OGTT:** The second postprandial 2-hour result was 10.13 mmol/L, classified as **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT)**.
- **Overall Trend:** Fasting glucose is improving, but postprandial glucose shows significant volatility.

**Simply put: You haven't reached the level of diabetes yet, but you are on the road toward it.**
The good news is: **This road can still be reversed.**

---

## 2. Analyzing Your Data Point by Point

### 2.1 Fasting Blood Sugar: From 7.05 to 5.71, A Positive Trend
Your fasting blood sugar progression: 7.05 → 5.58 → 5.47 → 5.02 → 5.45 → 5.71

**Interpretation:**
- **Initial value (7.05 mmol/L):** Just over the diagnostic standard for diabetes (≥7.0 mmol/L).
- **Gradual decline to 5.02 mmol/L:** Indicates effective lifestyle intervention.
- **Latest value (5.71 mmol/L):** Slightly above the ideal level (<5.6 mmol/L), but still within the normal range.

### 2.2 OGTT: Postprandial Blood Sugar is the Issue
Standard for OGTT 2-hour postprandial blood sugar:
- **Normal:** <7.8 mmol/L
- **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT):** 7.8-11.0 mmol/L ← *Your second result (10.13) falls in this range*
- **Diabetes:** ≥11.1 mmol/L

**Your three results: 5.62 / 10.13 / 6.84**
