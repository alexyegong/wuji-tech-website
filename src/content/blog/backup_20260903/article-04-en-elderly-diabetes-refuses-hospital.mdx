# Mom Refuses Hospital Treatment for Diabetes? How to Communicate with Elderly Parents Who Fear Seeking Care

> **Core Insight:** This isn't "stubbornness"; it's fear—fear of cost, fear of a diagnosis, fear of losing dignity. Understanding their fear is more effective than any logical argument.

---

## 1. Don't Rush to "Reason"—Why Won't They Go to the Hospital?

When you see your mother’s feet starting to ulcerate, you feel desperate, wanting to drag her to the hospital immediately. But you find her unwilling to go, offering various excuses:

- "Why waste that money? Just apply some ointment."
- "Hospitals just want your money; they won't cure it."
- "It’s a minor ailment; it’ll pass in a few days."
- "Don't fuss over me; I’m already at this age."

**If you argue, lecture, or present data at this moment, you will likely only make her more resistant.**

Because behind her refusal lie several deep-seated reasons you may have never truly understood:

### 1.1 Fear 1: Fear of Cost
This is the most realistic and common reason. For many elderly people who lived through times of scarcity, **"spending money on healthcare" is a huge psychological burden.**

In their mental model, **"Going to the hospital = spending money = wasting money."** Even if you have the ability to cover medical expenses, her internal sense of "poverty" will still drive her to refuse treatment.

### 1.2 Fear 2: Fear of a Serious Diagnosis
Many elderly people avoid the hospital because they are **afraid of the results**. Deep down, they suspect the ulcerated foot is not trivial. But if they don't go to the hospital, they can pretend it doesn't exist.

This is an **ostrich mentality**—burying their head in the sand and thinking the lion (the disease) doesn't exist.

### 1.3 Fear 3: Fear of Losing Autonomy
For the elderly, going to the hospital means admitting they are "old, sick, and useless." This **loss of dignity** is more frightening to them than the disease itself.
